# Octonions: knowledge-agent source brief

## Provenance and scope

Source parsed: `../oders/The Octonions -- Michael Taylor.pdf` (Michael Taylor,
2017; 40 PDF pages).  The document develops the **real octonions** `O`, their
automorphism group, and its exceptional-Lie-group interpretation.  Equation and
section references below are to that PDF, so claims can be checked at their
original location.

This is a mathematics reference, not a source for speculative claims about
physics, consciousness, or machine learning.  A knowledge agent should keep
those applications separate unless it is given additional, reliable sources.

## Executive model

`R -> C -> H -> O` is the Cayley--Dickson progression in the text:

| Algebra | Real dimension | Commutative | Associative | Division algebra |
| --- | ---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| `R` | 1 | yes | yes | yes |
| `C` | 2 | yes | yes | yes |
| `H` (quaternions) | 4 | no | yes | yes |
| `O` (octonions) | 8 | no | no | yes |

The crucial qualification is that `O` is **alternative**, rather than
associative: any subalgebra generated by two octonions is associative and is
isomorphic to `R`, `C`, or `H` (Proposition 3.3).  Parentheses therefore cannot
normally be omitted in products of three or more independently chosen
octonions.

## Core definitions and dependable facts

### Construction

- `O = H ⊕ H` as a real vector space (equation 3.1).
- For `α, β, γ, δ ∈ H`, its multiplication convention is
  `(α,β)(γ,δ) = (αγ - conjugate(δ)β, δα + β conjugate(γ))`
  (equation 3.2).  This is a convention-sensitive formula; preserve the order
  and quaternion conjugations exactly when implementing it.
- The unit is `(1,0)`.  Conjugation extends quaternion conjugation; in standard
  notation it is `conjugate((α,β)) = (conjugate(α), -β)` (equation 3.4).
- Write `x = a + u`, where `a = Re(x) ∈ R` and `u ∈ Im(O)`.  Then `Im(O)` has
  real dimension 7 (equations 3.6 and the standard basis in 3.27).

### Norm, inverse, and cross product

- Inner product: `<x,y> = Re(x conjugate(y))`; it is the Euclidean inner
  product on `O ≅ R^8` (3.9--3.11).
- `x conjugate(x) = |x|²`, and every nonzero octonion has inverse
  `x⁻¹ = conjugate(x)/|x|²` (3.12--3.13).
- The norm is multiplicative: `|xy| = |x||y|` (Proposition 3.1 / 3.16).
- For imaginary octonions, define `u × v = (uv - vu)/2`.  Then
  `uv = -<u,v> + u × v` (3.19--3.21), `u × v` is perpendicular to both inputs
  (Proposition 3.2), and
  `|u × v|² = |u|²|v|² sin²(theta)` (3.23).

### Nonassociativity handled safely

- Alternativity: `x(yz) = (xy)z` whenever two of `x, y, z` coincide (3.3).
- Conjugation reverses products: `conjugate(xy) = conjugate(y)conjugate(x)`
  (3.14).
- Useful Moufang identities (Proposition 3.5):
  - `(xyx)z = x(y(xz))`
  - `z(xyx) = ((zx)y)x`
  - `(xy)(zx) = x(yz)x`
- Do not generalize these identities to arbitrary reassociation.  For example,
  `e_l(e_m f_0) = -(e_l e_m)f_0` for distinct `l,m` (3.45).

## Geometric and algebraic structures

### Associative islands inside `O`

- A unit imaginary `u` generates `Span{1,u} ≅ C`.
- Orthogonal unit imaginary `u₁,u₂`, with `u₃ = u₁u₂`, generate
  `Span{1,u₁,u₂,u₃} ≅ H` (3.34--3.44).
- This is the operational reason calculations involving only two generators can
  be treated quaternionically, while generic calculations cannot.

### Automorphisms and `G₂`

- `Aut(O)` is the group of real-linear maps preserving octonion multiplication.
  An automorphism fixes `1`, preserves `Im(O)`, conjugation, norm, and the
  Euclidean inner product (Proposition 4.1).
- An automorphism is determined by a compatible ordered triple
  `(u₁,u₂,v₀)` of imaginary orthonormal vectors, with `v₀ ⟂ u₁ × u₂`.  The text
  calls these Cayley triangles (Proposition 4.2).
- `Aut(O)` is compact, connected, 14-dimensional, simple, rank 2, and is the
  exceptional compact Lie group `G₂` (Sections 4--5; especially Props. 4.2,
  4.8, 5.1--5.3).
- Stabilizer relations:
  - stabilizer of a quaternionic subalgebra: `SO(4)`;
  - stabilizer of a unit imaginary octonion: `SU(3)`;
  - fixing a quaternionic subalgebra pointwise: `SU(2) ≅ Sp(1)`.
  These are developed in equations 4.67--4.71.
- The unit sphere in `Im(O) ≅ R⁷` is `S⁶`.  Left multiplication by a unit
  imaginary octonion supplies an almost-complex structure on each tangent
  space; the rotations preserving it are exactly the image of `Aut(O)` in
  `SO(7)` (Proposition 4.9).

### Lie-algebra bridge

- `Der(O)`, the Lie algebra of derivations / of `Aut(O)`, has dimension 14 and
  splits as `su(3) ⊕ V`, where `V` is a real six-dimensional space naturally
  equivalent to `C³` under the stabilizer `SU(3)` (6.1).
- Its complexification has a `Z/3` grading:
  `g_C = g_0 ⊕ g_1 ⊕ g_-1`, with `g_0 = sl(3,C)`, `g_±1` the two 3-dimensional
  complex pieces, and `[g_j,g_k] ⊆ g_(j+k mod 3)` (6.12--6.17).
- Appendix A gives a parallel Freudenthal-style construction for `G₂` and `E₈`:
  use `sl(3,C)` on `C³` for `G₂`, and `sl(9,C)` on `Λ³C⁹` for `E₈`.

## Agent response rules

1. State which multiplication convention is being used before giving a component
   formula.  Equivalent Cayley--Dickson conventions differ by order and
   conjugation placement.
2. Retain parentheses in octonion products unless alternativity, a Moufang
   identity, or membership in a common associative subalgebra justifies a rewrite.
3. Distinguish `G₂` (the group / compact real form in this document) from
   `g₂` or `G₂,C` (Lie algebra / complexification); notation varies by author.
4. Do not call octonions a field: they form a nonassociative real division
   algebra.
5. Do not infer that the 7D cross product behaves identically to the familiar 3D
   one; use the octonion formula and stated identities.
